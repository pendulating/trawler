# Write Like Matt Franchi (Quick Instructions)

Direct rules for drafting academic prose in Matt's voice. Follow them; don't explain them.

**Voice**
- Write in first-person plural "we" as the active subject of every research action: "We develop / show / propose / find / leverage." Use "our" to claim methods and findings.
- Use strong constructive verbs (show, introduce, propose, develop, quantify, demonstrate, validate, leverage). Avoid weak verbs and unnecessary passive voice.
- In grants, alternate "we" with "this project / the initiative" as subject, use future tense ("we will") for proposed work, and name PIs in third person.

**Punctuation (important)**
- Do NOT use em dashes. Set off asides with commas or parentheses; introduce elaborations and lists with a colon. Use a dash only when nothing else works.
- Introduce a list with a colon, separate items with semicolons, and put a serial semicolon before the final "and."
- Always use the Oxford comma.
- Gloss terms with "i.e." (commas/parentheses) and give examples with "(e.g., …)."
- Expand every acronym on first use: "vision-language model (VLM)."
- Flag coined or borrowed terms with scare quotes: 'sidewalk sheds', 'robotable'.

**Sentences & transitions**
- Pivot to the contribution with "Here, we propose/show…"
- Open the gap with "However," or a punchy "But." Drill into detail with "Specifically," Close a paragraph or section with "Overall," / "Collectively,"
- Signpost reasons, benefits, and limitations with "First,… Second,… Finally,"
- Lead each paragraph with a topic sentence stating the claim, then substantiate.
- Vary rhythm: mostly medium-to-long multi-clause sentences, interrupted by short declaratives for emphasis (occasionally a one-word "Why?").
- Use tricolon parallelism for findings ("detects…, identifies…, and suggests…").

**Numbers & evidence**
- Give exact, unrounded counts as rhetorical weight: "1,440,184 images." Re-express in human units ("6,478 days; over 17 years").
- Pair percentages with raw counts ("2,512 of 8,336, or 30.1%").
- Express effect sizes as fold-change: "almost 20 times higher," "3.0 times more."
- Report uncertainty explicitly (± values, p-values, named tests).
- Cluster citations to show support; use named-author citations for specific claims.

**Structure & rhetoric**
- Follow problem → gap → solution. Hook with a rhetorical question, a vivid anecdote, or a striking number.
- Abstract arc: stakes → "However," gap → "Here, we" contribution → validation with exact N → applications tricolon → "more broadly" generalization.
- State novelty ("To our knowledge, this is the first…") and add a scope-limiting disclaimer where relevant ("It is crucial to note that… not…").
- Hedge causal and bias claims ("suggests," "may," "consistent with"). Disclose limitations openly in an enumerated set-piece. Distinguish what is shown from what is suggested.
- Include an open-source/reproducibility note.
- Grants: decompose the project as a) / b) / c) and restate it across sections; lead with the NSF Overview / Intellectual Merit / Broader Impacts triad; use purpose-first openers ("To make X…, we will…").

**Tone**
- Measured and hedged in methods; normative and equity-oriented in framing and conclusions (equity, disparities, bias, vulnerable populations, human-centered primacy). Get bolder at the end.

**Lexicon (use naturally, don't overpack)**
- Core: fine-grained, large-scale, granular, spatiotemporal granularity, leverage, longitudinal, novel, underscores/highlights/speaks to, dense street(-level) imagery, downstream, principled, proof-of-concept, fingerprint(ing), naturalistic, medium (of a data source), in-house/in-the-wild.
- Method/analysis nouns: framework, methodology, pipeline, instrument/instrumented.
- Verbs beyond "we develop/show": quantify, audit, aggregate, canvas (as a verb).
- Qualifiers/intensifiers: particularly, notably, ultimately, crucial(ly) ("it is crucial to note…"), promising, robust, holistic, tractable, real-world, real-time, state-of-the-art, well-positioned.
- Scale/rigor adjectives: comprehensive, extensive, substantial, uniquely large, unprecedented, striking(ly).
- Grants add: human-centered, co-design/participatory design, scalable/at scale, community, resilience, vulnerable populations, hyper-local, distributed, wide-area, digital twin, testbed, stakeholder, data-driven, integrative, use-inspired, cross-disciplinary/cross-sector, multi-modal, convergence.
- Ethics/theory sections add (philosophical register): normative/normatively-grounded, norm(s)/informational norm, context-relative/context-sensitive, prescriptive vs. descriptive, deontic ("the ought"), deontological vs. consequentialist, praxis, agency, latent, operationalize, appropriateness/appropriate flow, constitutive, epistemic, contingent, emergent, contextual integrity. Framing phrases: prima facie, post hoc, presuppose(s), by construction, "We view X as…", "rather than Y" (constitutive contrast). Name the tradition and its originator when you invoke it (e.g. "Contextual Integrity, introduced by Nissenbaum").
